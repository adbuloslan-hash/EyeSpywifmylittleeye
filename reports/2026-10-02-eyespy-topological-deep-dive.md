# EyeSpy — Technology, Surveillance, Influence & Mental Health
**Evidence-first topological deep dive — 2026-10-02**

## Executive finding
Modern phones, computers, platforms, data brokers, advertising systems, biometric tools, and government procurement form a documented surveillance-and-influence ecosystem. Public evidence establishes extensive data collection, profiling, algorithmic content selection, facial recognition, and public-sector acquisition of private AI capabilities.

The sources reviewed do **not** establish literal remote control of thoughts, a unified program manufacturing apocalyptic beliefs, or that mental illness as a whole was fabricated to keep people obedient. This project therefore separates demonstrable mechanisms from hypotheses that still require evidence.

## Topology
### 1. Device and interaction layer
Phones and computers can generate location, device, browsing, engagement, contact, image, audio, biometric, and behavioral signals depending on hardware, permissions, apps, and services.

### 2. Commercial surveillance layer
The FTC's 2024 staff report found extensive collection, retention, sharing, profiling, and monetization practices among major social-media and video-streaming services, including information obtained from data brokers and information about users and non-users.

### 3. Ranking, targeting, and persuasion
Recommendation systems, targeted advertising, notifications, interface design, and engagement optimization can influence attention and behavior. This is a concrete and testable form of behavioral influence; it should not be conflated with literal control of a person's thoughts.

### 4. Government–technology interface
GAO has documented federal use of facial recognition, including access to non-federal systems. GAO also reported in 2026 that federal agencies increasingly acquire AI capabilities from private companies. This creates a real public-private topology worth auditing for procurement, privacy, statutory authority, oversight, and mission creep.

### 5. Feedback loop
Interaction → data → profile/model → ranking/targeting → changed exposure/behavior → more interaction data.

## Mental health: structural critique without erasing illness
There are legitimate questions about diagnostic boundaries, institutional incentives, pharmaceutical and insurance economics, coercive treatment history, unequal access, and the medicalization of distress. But current clinical and epidemiological evidence does not support the universal claim that mental disorders are fabricated.

WHO identifies clinically significant disturbances in cognition, emotional regulation, or behavior associated with distress or impairment, while also emphasizing that poverty, violence, inequality, housing, employment, education, power, money, and other structural conditions shape mental health. A rigorous model therefore examines biological, psychological, social, economic, and institutional layers together.

## Claim-status ledger
| Claim | Status from reviewed evidence |
|---|---|
| Commercial platforms conduct extensive surveillance/data monetization | Established |
| Algorithms and targeted systems can influence attention/behavior | Established in ordinary persuasion/recommendation contexts |
| Federal agencies use facial recognition and acquire private AI | Established |
| Socioeconomic conditions materially affect mental health | Established |
| Institutional incentives can produce overreach or harmful practices | Case-specific; requires evidence |
| Phones/computers literally control thoughts | Not established |
| Mental illness is wholly fabricated | Contradicted as a universal claim by current clinical evidence |
| A unified government-tech program manufactures end-of-world beliefs | Not established |
